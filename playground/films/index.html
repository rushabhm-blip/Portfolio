What I learned in front of the camera

  • Small reads big. In a close shot a glance carries more than a gesture, so I learned to underplay.
  • Continuity is part of the performance. Same action, same prop, same pace on every take, or the cut breaks.
  • Act for the edit, not the order. Scenes are shot out of sequence, so I had to hold the feeling between setups.
  • The frame sets the stage. Hitting marks and moving within the shot taught me how much the camera decides.

02

Overheard

Storyboard / Camera / Edit

A shared memory. An unexpected listener.

“Aura” lets you share thoughts, scents and memories. It makes Anushka’s day feel close, until a late walk home shows who else is listening.

How we made it

  1. Script. 12 scenes in about 90 seconds: a calm morning, a library at night, footsteps on the walk home, and a twist in the lift.
  2. Storyboard. Every shot sketched before we filmed: the framing, the movement and what the audience should notice first.
  3. Camera. Calm push-ins for the morning. POV and handheld once the footsteps start. A tilt reveal in the lift, and an extreme close-up for the last beat.
  4. Edit. Long, easy takes early on, then faster cuts as fear builds. Sound carries the tension, and it all ends on a smash to black.

What I learned behind the camera

  • Plan the shot before you shoot it. The storyboard saved time on set and settled debates before they started.
  • Movement is emotion. A steady frame feels safe. Handheld and POV made the audience feel followed.
  • Sound does half the scaring. Footsteps, the Aura chime and the final glitch built tension the picture alone couldn’t.
  • The edit is the last rewrite. Pace and order change meaning, and sometimes a good shot has to go.
  • Film is a team sport. Four of us shared roles, so clear shot lists and quick decisions mattered as much as ideas.
